\section{Higher-Order}
\label{sec:higher}

Our investigation does not stop at second order: many datatypes of interest
have even higher kinds. Examples include monad transformers such as
\[
\AgdaKeyword{data}\;\AgdaDatatype{MaybeTrans}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{X}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;
\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type},
\]
the free monad generated by a container
\[
\AgdaKeyword{data}\;\AgdaDatatype{Free}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{SP}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaRecord{Cont}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{X}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;
\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type},
\]
and even the W-type itself:
\[
\AgdaKeyword{data}\;\AgdaDatatype{W}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{SP}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaRecord{Cont}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;
\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}.
\]

In this section, we introduce the syntax and semantics of higher-order
containers, and then turn to the question of higher-order W-types. We sketch an
argument showing that, in the present setting, W-types beyond
\AgdaFunction{2W} cannot exist.

\paragraph*{Normal Forms, Neutral Terms and Spines}

Rather than defining the syntax of higher-order containers directly, we first
define, by mutual induction, a syntax of \emph{normal forms}, \emph{neutral
terms} and \emph{spines}:

\input{code/Nf}

We begin with \AgdaRecord{Ne}, since it is the most ``contianer`` definition:
it consists of a type of shapes \AgdaField{S}; for each
variable in the context, a family of positions \AgdaField{P}; and, for each
variable and each of its positions, a family of applications \AgdaField{R}
describing the arguments need to be applied after a function variable.

A spine \AgdaDatatype{Sp} is essentially a list of normal forms
\AgdaDatatype{Nf}, which serves as the sequence of arguments applied to a
(possibly unsaturated) variable of function kind. More precisely, a spine
\AgdaBound{ts}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaDatatype{Sp}\;\AgdaBound{Γ}\;\AgdaBound{A}\;\AgdaBound{B}
turns a variable of kind \AgdaBound{A} into a term of kind \AgdaBound{B} by
applying it successively to the arguments in \AgdaBound{ts}. Since neutral
terms only appear in normal forms at kind \AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}, in
practice one always has
\AgdaBound{B}\;\AgdaSymbol{=}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}.

A normal form \AgdaDatatype{Nf} is either a neutral term
\AgdaInductiveConstructor{ne} or a $λ$-abstraction
\AgdaInductiveConstructor{lam}. Consequently, local $λ$-abstractions may occur
inside a definition; for instance:
\[
\begin{array}{l}
\AgdaFunction{H}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;
\AgdaSymbol{((}\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\\
\AgdaFunction{H}\;\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaSymbol{=}\;
\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaSymbol{(λ}\;\AgdaBound{X}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaRecord{⊤}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaDatatype{⊎}}\;\AgdaBound{X}\AgdaSymbol{)}
\end{array}
\]

This structure is strongly inspired by the standard presentation of
$β$-normal, $η$-long forms of the simply typed $λ$-calculus. This design
allows hereditary substitution of variables to be defined in a structurally
recursive way.

\paragraph*{Category of Higher-Order Containers}

For every \AgdaBound{A}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaDatatype{Kind}, higher-order
containers of kind \AgdaBound{A} form a category. A morphism consists of three
components: a map on shapes; for each shape and each variable, a map on the
positions of that variable in the reverse direction; and, for each shape and
each position of each variable, a family of morphisms between the arguments
(spines) to which that variable is applied. Formally, the morphisms of
\AgdaDatatype{Nf}, \AgdaRecord{Ne} and \AgdaDatatype{Sp} are defined by mutual
induction, and the morphisms of \AgdaFunction{HCont} are obtained as the
special case of the empty context.

\paragraph*{Higher-Order W-Types?}

However, in the present setting, W-types cannot exist beyond second order,
since their existence would allow us to construct the following
counterexample. Suppose the general higher-order W-types exist, that is, we \AgdaKeyword{postulate}
\[ \AgdaFunction{HW}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaFunction{HCont}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{A}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}\;\AgdaBound{A}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaFunction{HCont}\;\AgdaBound{A}
\]

Within the calculus, we can define the signature of the ordinary W-type as a
higher-order container:
\[
\begin{array}{l}
\AgdaFunction{SigWᶜ}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;
\AgdaFunction{HCont}\;\AgdaSymbol{(((}\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\AgdaSymbol{)}\\
\AgdaFunction{SigWᶜ}\;\AgdaSymbol{=}\;
\AgdaFunction{nf}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaInductiveConstructor{lam}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaInductiveConstructor{lam}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaFunction{app}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaFunction{var}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{vz}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaFunction{app}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaFunction{var}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaInductiveConstructor{vs}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{vz}\AgdaSymbol{))}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaFunction{var}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{vz}\AgdaSymbol{)))))}
\end{array}
\]
that is, \AgdaFunction{SigWᶜ} represents the operation
\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaBound{G}\;\AgdaSymbol{↦}\;\AgdaBound{G}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaBound{G}\AgdaSymbol{)}.
Combining the two, we obtain an \emph{internal} W-type:
\[
\begin{array}{l}
\AgdaFunction{Wᶜ}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;
\AgdaFunction{HCont}\;\AgdaSymbol{((}\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\AgdaSymbol{)}\\
\AgdaFunction{Wᶜ}\;\AgdaSymbol{=}\;\AgdaPostulate{HW}\;\AgdaFunction{SigWᶜ}
\end{array}
\]

Using the categorical interpretation, we obtain an \emph{external} W-type:
\[
\begin{array}{l}
\AgdaFunction{W}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;
\AgdaFunction{HCont}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaFunction{HCont}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\\
\AgdaFunction{W}\;\AgdaSymbol{=}\;\AgdaFunction{napp}\;\AgdaFunction{Wᶜ}
\end{array}
\]
Up to the equivalences
\AgdaFunction{HCont}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaInductiveConstructor{⇒}}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaSymbol{≃}\;\AgdaRecord{Cont}
and
\AgdaFunction{HCont}\;\AgdaInductiveConstructor{*}\;\AgdaSymbol{≃}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type},
the function \AgdaFunction{W} has type
\AgdaRecord{Cont}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type} and coincides with the
ordinary W-type defined inductively using Agda \emph{data}
\AgdaKeyword{data}\;\AgdaDatatype{W}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{SP}\;
\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaPostulate{Cont}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;
\AgdaPrimitive{Type}
. However, if we instead use the type-level interpretation, we obtain

\[
\begin{array}{l}
\AgdaFunction{W-bad}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;
\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\AgdaSymbol{)}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\\
\AgdaFunction{W-bad}\;\AgdaSymbol{=}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaFunction{⟦}}\;\AgdaFunction{Wᶜ}\;\AgdaOperator{\AgdaFunction{⟧}}
\end{array}
\]
which assigns a fixed point
\AgdaFunction{W-bad}\;\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaSymbol{≃}\;\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaFunction{W-bad}\;\AgdaBound{F}\AgdaSymbol{)}
to \emph{every} function
\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaSymbol{:}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaPrimitive{Type}.
This cannot hold, since fixed points are only guaranteed to exist for strictly
positive functors: taking
\AgdaBound{F}\;\AgdaBound{X}\;\AgdaSymbol{=}\;\AgdaBound{X}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaDatatype{⊥},
we would obtain a type \AgdaBound{D} with
\AgdaBound{D}\;\AgdaSymbol{≃}\;\AgdaSymbol{(}\AgdaBound{D}\;\AgdaSymbol{→}\;\AgdaDatatype{⊥}\AgdaSymbol{)},
from which \AgdaDatatype{⊥} is derivable. Hence we arrive at a contradiction.
